I am Milad Soleymani, a Master’s student in Artificial Intelligence and Robotics at Islamic Azad University (GPA 4.0/4.0), and an AI engineer. I am applying to Ph.D. programs.

The question that runs through my work is how large models can be adapted — efficiently, and without losing what makes them useful — to the settings they were not built for. A language other than English. A single patient’s brain. A clinical domain with almost no labelled data. Pretrained models are general in principle and surprisingly brittle in practice, and I am interested in why, and in what it takes to fix it.

That question has taken me across several areas rather than into one. I work on parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large vision and language models, on reinforcement learning for aligning what models generate with what people actually need, on self-supervised and contrastive representation learning for signals that come with few labels, and on evaluation — measuring what a model does rather than what it appears to do. Recently that has meant showing that a model’s architecture, not its prompt, decides whether it can reason in a low-resource language; aligning brain-signal encoders with language so a brain–computer interface can decode commands it was never trained on; and adapting vision transformers to medical imaging under real deployment constraints.

The application domains have been just as varied — EEG and brain–computer interfaces, medical imaging, clinical text, speech and audio — because the methods travel, and working across them is how I have learned which ideas are general and which only looked general. In industry I build the systems that keep this honest: retrieval-augmented pipelines, LLM agents, and the evaluation harnesses that decide between them on measured accuracy and cost rather than intuition.

For a Ph.D. I want to keep working at this intersection of efficient adaptation, representation learning, and rigorous evaluation of large models, whether the signal is language, vision, or neural data, and whether the goal is a better method or a system that survives contact with a real application. I am drawn to problems where the constraint — little data, little compute, a language or a population the model has never properly seen — is the interesting part rather than an obstacle to be apologised for.

My research and publications pages go into detail, and my full CV is available to download. I am always glad to hear from researchers working on related problems — the best way to reach me is by email.
