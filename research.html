Research

Multilingual Reasoning in Language Models

  1. Reasoning in Persian is optional: a compliance axis for target-language chain-of-thought

    May 2026 — Present · with Dr. Nava Eslami

    A model asked to think in Persian usually thinks in English instead. I found that whether it complies is decided by architecture, not by the prompt: on 210 paired MathMist problems, a model with a dedicated reasoning channel produced Persian chain-of-thought in only 4% of cases, against 80–85% for inline-CoT models, and disabling that thinking channel flipped the same weights to 84% (paired McNemar). The result also exposes a measurement hazard for cross-lingual evaluation: English accuracy falls 91% → 33% as Persian-CoT compliance rises — which means a model can look robust to non-English reasoning while having never reasoned in it at all.

    I built the evaluation harness behind the result (vLLM and API inference, sentence-level language identification, paired significance tests), and I am now extending it from inference time to training time: how the language of the chain-of-thought shapes GRPO training dynamics under verifiable rewards, tracking rollout language ratio and switch rate through training rather than final accuracy alone.

    Manuscript in preparation, 2026. Code available on request.

Representation Learning for Brain Signals

  1. Language-aligned and parameter-efficient decoding

    Oct 2025 — May 2026 · with Dr. Nava Eslami

    A brain–computer interface trained on fixed class labels can only ever decode the classes it was shown. I aligned an ATCNet brain-signal encoder with a frozen sentence-transformer text encoder using CLIP-style contrastive training, so motor tasks are decoded from natural-language descriptions rather than bare labels — a step toward zero-shot decoding of commands the model has never seen. Alongside it I built LoRA adapters for per-subject calibration (rank 8, ~1% of parameters), aimed at the cross-subject variability that keeps BCIs in the lab. Reinforcement-learning fine-tuning is underway, with the purpose of reliable cross-subject decoding from minimal calibration data.

  2. Task-agnostic embeddings and EEG classification

    Mar 2020 — Jan 2025 · with Dr. Farhad Goodarzy, Dr. Andishe Partovi

    Earlier work on the same problem from the self-supervised side: preprocessing and training pipelines across several public EEG datasets, learning embeddings that transfer across tasks, together with the benchmarking and time–frequency signal processing underneath. As a data scientist at KeyLead Health I contributed to two EEG projects that became peer-reviewed publications (Partovi et al., IEEE NER 2023; Journal of Neural Engineering 2025). My own first paper in this line — a deep learning algorithm for classifying grasp motions from multi-session EEG — came out of a 6th-place finish in the 2020 International BCI Competition.

Parameter-Efficient Adaptation for Medical AI

  1. PEFT for low-resource vision and language models

    Jan 2025 — Present · with Dr. Maliheh Sabeti, Dr. Nava Eslami

    Medical tasks rarely come with enough labelled data to fine-tune a large pretrained model end to end. I adapt large vision models (Swin, ViT) to specialist medical tasks by training a small subset of their parameters, cutting the compute needed to reach task performance. The concrete result is an efficient Swin Transformer architecture for cutaneous melanoma detection, submitted to Biomedical Signal Processing and Control.

    Manuscript available upon request.

Empathic and Affective Language Models

  1. Reinforcement learning for empathic generation

    Sep 2024 — Sep 2025

    Empathy is easy to ask a model for and hard to reward it for. I fine-tuned language models with Group Relative Policy Optimization against composite reward functions — a semantic-similarity term for staying on topic, and a fine-tuned RoBERTa empathy model as the reward signal itself — for patient-aligned generation in healthcare, alongside retrieval-augmented pipelines for medical domain tasks.

    GRPO-Empathy code

  2. Annotating clinical empathy

    2026

    Reward models for empathy are only as good as the construct they are trained on, so this is the data half of the problem: an annotation tool and schema for "breaking bad news" conversations, built on the Appraisal Framework for Clinical Empathy, for labelling the empathic moves clinicians actually make.
