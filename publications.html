  1. An Efficient Swin Transformer Architecture for Cutaneous Melanoma Detection

    2026

    Mohammad Reza Esmkhani, Milad Soleymani, Soroush Ziaee, Nava Eslami, Reza Boostani, Maliheh Sabeti
    Biomedical Signal Processing and Control (Elsevier)

    Melanoma is the deadliest form of skin cancer, and the models that detect it best are the ones hardest to deploy where they are needed. We develop and validate parameter-efficient Swin Transformer adapters for melanoma classification: bottleneck adapters with a down-projection, non-linearity and up-projection, training only a small fraction of the model's parameters while keeping the modularity that multi-task clinical settings require. Evaluated with stratified 4-fold cross-validation on established benchmarks, measuring sensitivity, specificity and F1 alongside inference time, memory footprint and FLOPs against full fine-tuning. The result is a framework aimed at resource-constrained deployment without giving up clinical-grade accuracy.

  1. Reasoning in Persian Is Optional: A Compliance Axis for Target-Language Chain-of-Thought in 8B Models

    2026

    Milad Soleymani, Kimia Hoseyni, Soroush Ziaee, Nava Eslami
    Manuscript in preparation

    Asked to reason in Persian, most open models reason in English instead. We show that compliance is decided by architecture rather than by the prompt: on 210 paired MathMist problems, a model with a dedicated reasoning channel produced Persian chain-of-thought in only 4% of cases, against 80–85% for inline-CoT models, and disabling that thinking channel flipped the same weights to 84% (paired McNemar). The result also exposes a measurement hazard for cross-lingual evaluation: English accuracy falls 91% → 33% as Persian-CoT compliance rises, so an apparently robust model may never have reasoned in Persian at all. We release the evaluation harness (vLLM and API inference, sentence-level language identification, paired significance tests) and extend the finding to training time with GRPO on Llama-3.1/3.2.

    Results complete, manuscript drafted, available upon request.

  1. A Deep Learning Algorithm for Classifying Grasp Motions using Multi-session EEG Recordings

    2021

    Andishe Partovi, Seyed Mehrshad Hosseini, Milad Soleymani, Kiana Liaghat, Soroush Ziaee, Erfan Habibi Panah Fard, Sina Sadeghpour Vajdi, Farhad Goodarzy
    9th International Winter Conference on Brain-Computer Interface (BCI)

    Classifying motor imagery from scalp EEG is hard enough. It is harder still when the gestures all belong to the same hand. We distinguish three grasp motions (cylindrical, spherical and lumbrical) across two-day training sessions in 15 subjects of a public dataset, using two ensembles — anomaly detection paired with a fully connected network, and with a convolutional network. The method reaches over 80% accuracy in 3 subjects and 57% on average across the cohort, supporting the feasibility of decoding fine motor intent from scalp EEG. The work grew out of a 6th-place finish in the 2020 International BCI Competition.
